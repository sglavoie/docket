# Retired keys

Every key an earlier vocabulary used and the sigils replaced, inventoried in one
place rather than left beside the surface that dropped it.

The operation descriptors are the single source for dispatch, contextual
guidance, chord hints, and the key rows of `?` help. Every key an earlier
vocabulary used and the sigils replaced is simply unbound, with no compatibility
action, and every surface's retirements are inventoried here rather than left
beside the surface that dropped them. The Outliner gave up the old standalone `b`, `c`, `m`, and
`w` actions, the comma state prefix, and `:`, `C`, `R`, and `Ctrl+Esc`. Two
names came back with new acts rather than as aliases: `t` is the task-subtree
fold and `P` is Select Project. `H`, `M`, and `L` went with the
screen-relative cursor jumps, because the `z` scroll chord moves the window
rather than the cursor and is the half of that pair with no other spelling. `M`
has since been spent: it is the menu bar toggle, a third name back with a new
act. `H` and `L` stay silent, and they are the pair that is not free to return,
since either one alone would advertise half of a jump the `z` chord already
owns.
`C` is a fourth name back with a new act: it appends a comment to the cursor
task without leaving the list, the write beside the read `;` already opens. `b`
is a fifth: it toggles Peek, and it is the one return that retired a key in
exchange, since `Ctrl+Enter` gave Peek up to it and is now silent with no alias
back. A letter earns a place beside `Enter` and `Shift+Enter`, which zoom and
widen, better than a modifier does.
`d` is unbound: Done stays `$d` alone, `@` answers both dates, and root `z` is
the scroll chord prefix. `$` joins them as a chord prefix rather than a key of
its own. Select Project holds the same line: `n` no longer creates a project,
`A` no longer clears a selection, and bare `g` is the go-to prefix rather than
cursor-top, so all three are silent there. All Notes retired `s`, `r`, `m`, `n`,
and `x` for `/`, `e`, `%`, `a`, and the `Space` sheet, and freed `v` rather than
reassigning it, because Notes has no selection for it to toggle; all six are
silent there. `r` is the one of the six spent since: it writes the cursor task's
resume note in the Outliner. A retirement is scoped to the surface that made it,
so the letter follows the act nowhere and stays silent in Notes. Attachments retired `d` and `x` for its `Space` sheet, `o`
for `Enter`, `e` for `E`, and `O`, `Ctrl+E`, and `Ctrl+O` for that same sheet;
all seven are silent there. `u` was retired with them and has since come back:
once unlink moved to that sheet the key was free, so it now means here what it
means everywhere else. `e` is the one that had to move
rather than retire outright: it means edit or rename on every other surface, and
a panel with nothing to rename may not spend it on the editor. The `f#` and `fc`
filter pickers are the same rule read the other way: their rows are stored names,
so `e` renames one there and means exactly what it means elsewhere. Comments retired
the hidden `n` add alias for `a`, and `d` and `x` for its `Space` sheet; all
three are silent there, because a comment may not stay one press from deletion
while a task cannot be deleted that way. One rule covers that whole inventory.
Pressing an unfinished chord and then a key it does not offer names the path you
left, as `$q is not a workflow action`; pressing a key that was never bound does
nothing and says nothing, because it never promised anything.
Review answers to `=` on every surface: the sigil `=review` writes the flag in a
capture line, the `=` key toggles it on a row, and the `f=` facet narrows by it.
The letter `fr` that facet once answered to is retired. `$review` stays a
rejected destination, so no `$` terminal could honor it: a destination is
singular, and a task flagged for review still has a lifecycle state.
